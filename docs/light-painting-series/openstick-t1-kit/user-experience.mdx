> Zero-threshold — no drawing skills needed, no complex parameters. Follow along and get great light painting shots the first time.

Many people worry when trying light painting for the first time — "I'm not good at drawing," "I don't know how to compose." With the T1, there's no need to worry. You don't need drawing skills or a good sense of spatial composition. Just pick a pattern, press the shutter, and walk steadily — the shot is done.

---

## 📸 Preparation Before Shooting

Just prepare these few things before shooting:

1. **Environment**: Find a dark place — outdoors at night or a room without lights. The darker it is, the cleaner the light painting will look
2. **Stabilize camera/phone**: Use a tripod, or place your phone/camera on a stable surface. Long exposure blurs if the camera shakes
3. **Pick a pattern**: Power on and select the pattern you want to shoot. Note how long it takes to play (the screen shows the duration)
4. **Position yourself**: Stand in front of the camera, wand facing the lens, and plan your start and end positions

---

## ⚙️ How to Set Parameters (Super Simple)

No complex formulas needed — just one core rule:

> **Shutter Speed = Pattern Playback Duration + 1~2 seconds**

For example, if your pattern takes 8 seconds to play, set the shutter to 9-10 seconds. The extra 1-2 seconds gives you time to press the shutter and start the wand, ensuring the shot doesn't end halfway.

Other parameter references:

- **Phone**: Open pro mode/long exposure mode, set ISO to 100-200, focus to infinity
- **Camera**: Manual mode, ISO 100, aperture f/8-f/11, set shutter as described above
- If the shot is too bright: Lower ISO or narrow the aperture
- If the shot is too dark: Increase wand brightness or slightly raise ISO

---

## 🎬 Shooting Steps (3 Steps to Perfect Shots)

1. **Press the shutter**: Start with your camera/phone shutter to begin exposure
2. **Start the light painting**: Press the wand button to start pattern playback, then walk steadily from left to right (or right to left). Keep the wand vertical, and match your walking speed to the pattern playback speed
3. **Finish**: When the pattern finishes playing and you've reached your position, wait for the shutter to close — your shot is done

It's that simple. Try a couple times to find the right walking speed, and you'll have a high success rate on your first attempt.

## 💡 Tips for Great Shots

### 1. Don't Limit Yourself to Fixed Patterns

Beyond shooting preset patterns, you can also:

- **Spin the wand**: Slowly rotate the wand for abstract, artistic light trails
- **Draw circles or move up and down**: Any movement creates beautiful light effects
- **Portrait shots**: Have your model stand still while you paint light around them from behind or the side for creative portrait effects

### 2. Move Steadily

You don't need to walk fast — just maintain a constant speed. Keep your hand steady and the wand vertical, and your patterns won't appear distorted.

### 3. Experiment with Brightness

Use lower brightness in dark environments, higher brightness for large scenes or when farther from the lens. Different brightness levels create different moods.

## 🎨 No Custom Materials? No Problem!

Tired of preset patterns and want your own content? It's easy:

- **No design skills**: Use AI image tools (like Midjourney, DALL-E) to generate your desired images — text, patterns, silhouette portraits work. Adjust to high-contrast black and white, then transfer to the wand
- **Familiar with PS/editing**: Create more detailed custom materials yourself — text, logos, complex patterns. Official tutorials are available for material creation
- **Transfer is simple**: Connect your phone via Wi-Fi and tap to transfer — no complex operations needed
