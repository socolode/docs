# Post-Processing: Local Color Grading for Light Painting

> No complex Photoshop needed — 5 minutes on your phone is enough to take your light painting photos from "okay" to "stunning."

Many people find their light painting photos look hazy, the light lacks pop, colors are dull, and the background is messy. You don't need to reshoot — a quick edit can make a huge difference.

Light painting post-processing is different from regular photos. The key is **local adjustments**: brighten and saturate the light, darken and clean the background. Separate the two, and the photo instantly becomes crisp and clear.

Use any mobile editing app (醒图, Polarr, Lightroom mobile — all work), 5 minutes is all you need. No computer required.

---

## 🎨 Step 1: Basic Adjustments — Clean Up the Photo

Global adjustments to remove the haze:

1. **Exposure**: Don't push it too high or the light will blow out. Just enough to keep the shadows from going completely black
2. **Contrast**: +15 to +25 — immediately separates the light from the background
3. **Highlights**: -20 to -30 — prevents the brightest parts of the light from blowing out, preserves detail
4. **Shadows**: -10 to -20 — darkens the background, removes color noise
5. **Blacks**: -10 to -15 — makes the black background purer, cleaner
6. **Clarity/Sharpening**: +10 to +15 — sharper light edges, clearer patterns

After this step, the photo already looks much cleaner.

---

## ✨ Step 2: The Core! Local Adjustments — Make the Light "Pop"

This is the most important step — the soul of light painting editing. Use the "Local Adjustment," "Mask," or "Brush" tool in your app:

1. **Select the light area**: Paint over the light trails with the brush, avoiding the background
2. **Apply these adjustments to the light only**:
   - Brightness/Exposure: +10 to +20 — makes the light brighter and more transparent
   - Saturation: +20 to +30 — colors instantly become vibrant, no more dull gray
   - Sharpening: +15 — sharper light edges, crisper text and patterns
   - If the color is off, adjust white balance/tint separately — cooler blues or warmer yellows
3. **Now select the background**: Invert the selection or paint the background
   - Brightness: -10 to -20 — darken the background
   - Saturation: -10 to -20 — removes stray colors from the background, letting the light stand out
   - White balance: slightly cooler — purer black background

After this, the light will look like it's emerging from the black background — crisp, transparent, and textured.

---

## 🌈 Step 3: Styling — Dial in the Mood You Want

Once the basics are done, add a color tone to match your style:

- **Cyberpunk**: Add blue and purple to the light, cyan to the shadows, high contrast — full futuristic vibe
- **Warm Retro**: Add orange and yellow to the light, brown to the shadows, yellow to the highlights — cozy atmosphere
- **Minimal Cool**: Overall blue tint, slightly desaturated — premium, sophisticated
- **Dreamy Soft Glow**: Add a subtle glow/bloom effect to the light — soft, ethereal halos

Don't overdo filters — they'll overpower the light's natural color. Less is more.

---

## ❌ Post-Processing Pitfalls

1. **Don't increase global saturation**: It will bring out all the background noise too. Always adjust locally.
2. **Don't brighten the light too much**: Blown-out light loses all detail. Pull the highlights down.
3. **Don't oversharpen**: Too much sharpening creates noise and white edges around the light. Moderation is key.
4. **Don't slap on random filters**: The light is the star. Heavy filters steal the show.
5. **A good original shot is still the foundation**: Post-processing enhances, it doesn't rescue. A well-captured original will look great with minimal editing.
