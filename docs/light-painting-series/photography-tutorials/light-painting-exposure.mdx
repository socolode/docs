# Exposure Core: One Formula for Light Painting & Background

> Stop guessing settings — understand how these parameters work together and you'll nail the exposure in any scene.

The most frustrating thing for beginners is exposure: light too bright and blown out, background pitch black, or the background is bright but the light is gone. Light painting exposure is actually simple — understand what each parameter does, remember one formula, and you'll get it right every time.

---

## 📐 First: What Does Each Parameter Control?

There are only three parameters, and each does a different job:

| Parameter | What it controls | How to use in light painting |
|-|-|-|
| **Shutter Speed** | How long you have to draw, how much light hits the background | Set it to how long you need to draw (1-2 seconds longer than the pattern duration); longer for a brighter background, shorter for a darker one |
| **ISO** | How sensitive the sensor is to light | Lower = better quality, higher = brighter but more noise; use ISO 100-400, try not to exceed 800 |
| **Aperture** | How much light enters, how thick the light trail looks | Wider aperture (smaller f-number) = thicker, brighter light; narrower aperture (larger f-number) = finer, sharper light; typically f/5.6-f/11 |

---

## 🧮 The Core Exposure Formula

Remember this one rule:

> **Background brightness = shutter speed; Light brightness = aperture / light wand brightness; Image quality = ISO**

In plain English:

1. **Want a brighter/darker background?** Adjust the shutter speed. Longer shutter = more light hitting the background = brighter. Want it dark? Shorten the shutter.
2. **Want brighter/dimmer light?** Adjust the aperture or the light wand's brightness. Light brightness has nothing to do with shutter speed (the light is always moving), so don't try to brighten the light by extending the shutter — you'll just overexpose the background.
3. **Keep ISO as low as possible**: ISO 100 gives the best quality. Only raise it if it's really dark. High ISO introduces noise.

## 🎯 Scene Presets — Copy These Directly

### Scene 1: Pure black background (dark room / suburb with no lights)

- **Shutter**: Set to how long you need to draw (e.g., 9-10 seconds for an 8-second pattern)
- **ISO**: 100
- **Aperture**: f/8
- **Result**: Pure black background, crisp and bright light — the cleanest look

### Scene 2: Outdoor at night, want some environment visible (buildings, trees, sky)

- **Shutter**: 2-3 seconds longer than the drawing time (e.g., 11-13 seconds for 8 seconds of drawing)
- **ISO**: 100-200
- **Aperture**: f/5.6-f/8
- **Result**: Bright light with visible surroundings — adds environmental context

### Scene 3: Light painting portraits (need to capture the person clearly)

- **Shutter**: 10-15 seconds — enough time to draw and expose the person
- **ISO**: 200-400
- **Aperture**: f/4-f/5.6
- **Tip**: Use a flash or fill light for 1 second just before the shutter closes to freeze the person without affecting the light trail

### Scene 4: Shooting at dusk (sky not fully dark)

- **Shutter**: 5-8 seconds — don't go too long or the sky will overexpose
- **ISO**: 100
- **Aperture**: f/8-f/11
- **Light wand brightness**: Turn it up so the light stands out against the sky

## ❌ Common Exposure Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Light too bright, blown out, no detail

**Cause**: Aperture too wide, light wand too bright, or ISO too high
**Fix**: Stop down the aperture (higher f-number), dim the light wand, drop ISO to 100

### Mistake 2: Background pitch black, nothing visible

**Cause**: Shutter speed too short, not enough light hitting the background
**Fix**: Extend shutter by 2-3 seconds or raise ISO slightly. Don't adjust aperture to fix this — the light will get brighter too

### Mistake 3: Background is bright but light is also overexposed

**Cause**: You tried to brighten the light by extending the shutter, which also brightened the background
**Fix**: Adjust aperture or light wand brightness for the light. Don't touch the shutter — it controls the background, not the light

### Mistake 4: Lots of noise, grainy photo

**Cause**: ISO too high
**Fix**: Keep ISO at 100-200, never above 800. If it's too dark, brighten the light wand or open the aperture

---

## 💡 Exposure Tips

1. **Take a test shot first**: Before the real thing, snap a test shot with your settings to check light brightness and background. Adjust if needed.
2. **Shoot in RAW**: If your camera supports RAW, use it. You'll have much more room to adjust exposure in post-processing.
3. **Underexpose rather than overexpose**: Slightly dim light can be brightened in post, but blown-out highlights are gone forever. When in doubt, go a bit darker.
4. **Adjust the wand brightness first**: If the light is too bright or too dim, adjust the light wand itself first — it's easier than changing camera settings and won't affect the background.
